# ORDIQ Usability and Evaluation Questionnaire

The college asked for a usability questionnaire for the pilot test (answer 19). This questionnaire has two parts:

- **Part A** is the System Usability Scale (SUS; Brooke, 1996), a standard 10-item questionnaire that is free to use and widely cited. Its score can be compared with published benchmarks.
- **Part B** covers the college's own success measures: time saved in preparing the schedule, and conflicts or errors found after the schedule reaches the university portal.

**Respondents:** the people in the Dean's office who used ORDIQ during the pilot. They answer once, after preparing a schedule with ORDIQ.

**Ethics:** participation is voluntary, answers are anonymous, and taking part or declining has no effect on employment.

### Scoring

1. For odd-numbered items (1, 3, 5, 7, 9), subtract 1 from the answer.
2. For even-numbered items (2, 4, 6, 8, 10), subtract the answer from 5.
3. Add the ten results and multiply the sum by 2.5. The score runs from 0 to 100.

A SUS score is not a percentage. Scores are usually compared with the published average of about 68: above 68 is above average usability, and roughly 80 or more is in the top range. With few respondents, report each score and the mean, not only the mean.

## Reporting

- Report the SUS scores (Part A) as usability.
- Compare B1 with B2, and B3 with the errors returned for the previous semester's schedule, as the time-saved and conflict-reduction results. ORDIQ's evaluation records (effort log, corrections and test records) give matching figures from inside the system.
- Report B5 to B8 item by item (count of answers per box, plus the median), since a few respondents do not support averages.
